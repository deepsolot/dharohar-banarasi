Heirloom Preservation

How to Care for Your Banarasi Silk Saree

Pure Banarasi silk is an investment in artisanal craftsmanship. With proper care, these handwoven masterpieces retain their luster and zari strength for decades, passing gracefully from one generation to the next.

🧼

1. Washing & Cleaning

Strictly Dry Clean Only: Always dry clean pure Katan, Organza, Tanchoi, and Georgette Banarasi sarees. Water washing can tarnish genuine zari threads and cause pure mulberry silk to lose its natural sheen.

  • Choose a trusted, experienced dry cleaner familiar with real silk and zari.
  • Avoid petrol washes or harsh chemical bleaches.
  • For minor liquid spills, immediately blot gently with a dry white cotton cloth without rubbing.
🧺

2. Storage & Wrapping

Use Breathable Muslin Cloth: Never store silk in synthetic plastic bags, which trap moisture and can react chemically with metallic zari threads.

  • Wrap each saree individually in unbleached cotton muslin cloth or soft mulmul.
  • Store flat on a wooden or cloth-lined shelf, away from direct sunlight and dampness.
  • Use natural dried neem leaves or cloves instead of naphthalene balls to prevent chemical discoloration.
🔄

3. Refolding Routine

Change Folds Periodically: Storing heavy zari sarees in the exact same fold for long durations can weaken silk yarns along crease lines.

  • Unfold and change the crease lines every 3 to 4 months.
  • Air out the saree in a well-ventilated room in gentle shade for 20 minutes before refolding.
  • Fold with the zari work facing inward to protect against accidental friction or snagging.
♨️

4. Ironing & Steaming

Low Heat & Protective Buffer: Never apply a hot iron directly onto the saree face or zari motifs.

  • Set the iron to the lowest 'Silk' setting.
  • Always iron on the reverse side or place a fine cotton cloth over the saree.
  • Do not spray water directly on silk while ironing, as this can cause water ring marks.
✨

5. Perfume & Sweat Protection

Keep Scents at a Distance: Spritzing perfumes, deodorants, or hairsprays directly on zari causes rapid silver/gold oxidation and dark tarnishing.

  • Apply perfume and body lotion and let it dry completely before draping your saree.
  • Use underarm sweat pads if wearing in hot or humid conditions.
  • Air the saree immediately after wear before placing it back into storage.
🧵

6. Fall, Pico & Roll Press

Professional Finishing: Adding a high-quality cotton fall provides weight and structure, safeguarding the lower hemline from heel abrasion.

  • Stitch a 100% pre-washed cotton fall along the pleats and bottom border.
  • Get pico (overlocking) done along both pallu ends to prevent thread fraying.
  • Request traditional steam roll-pressing from silk specialists to maintain the crisp fall.

GI Certified Craft

5 Hallmarks of an Authentic Handloom Banarasi Saree

Varanasi handweaving is protected by Geographical Indication (GI). Here is how you can verify genuine handloom purity against powerloom imitations.

01

The Floating Weft (Kadwa vs Kadiyal Technique)

On genuine handloom Banarasi sarees, turn the saree over to inspect the reverse side. In authentic handwoven Kadwa weaving, each booti motif is individually crafted with separate shuttles, leaving neat, soft floating threads without harsh plastic mesh. Powerlooms cut threads mechanically, resulting in stiff, scratchy patches.

02

The Loom Pin Marks on the Selvedge

Look closely at the border edge (selvedge) of the saree. Authentic handloom sarees feature tiny, evenly spaced pinholes or shuttle clip marks where the warp threads were pinned to the wooden pit-loom beam during weeks of weaving. Machine-woven sarees have sharp, cold laser-cut synthetic edges.

03

Warm Metallic Luster of Tested Zari

Authentic Banarasi uses electroplated gold and silver zari on silk/metallic core threads that impart a rich, muted, regal warmth. Cheap synthetic imitations exhibit an unnaturally bright, glossy plastic-like yellow glare that turns black or flakes easily.

04

Silk Mark & GI (Geographical Indication) Proof

Dharohar Banarasi works under the aegis of Choudhary Saree Industries (Est. 1979). Our pure silk creations comply with Silk Mark guidelines guaranteeing 100% natural mulberry and katan silk filaments with zero polyester blending.

05

The Weight, Drape & Touch of Pure Katan

Pure mulberry Katan silk possesses a distinctive natural hand-feel — crisp yet soft, cool to the touch, and surprisingly light despite rich zari density. When rubbed gently between fingers, genuine silk produces a faint rustling sound known as the 'scroop' of silk.

Styling Masterclass

Step-by-Step Banarasi Saree Draping Styles

Elevate your presence with traditional and contemporary ways to showcase the opulent zari pallu and regal border of your Banarasi saree.

Timeless Classic

1. Classic Nivi Drape (Royal Pleated)

The universally flattering traditional drape that showcases the pleats cleanly and lets the heavy zari pallu rest gracefully over the left shoulder.

Step 1

Tuck the plain inner end into the petticoat waistband, starting at navel and completing one full clockwise circle around your waist.

Step 2

Measure the pallu length (keep at least 1 to 1.2 meters to showcase the full Banarasi zari jaal) and bring it across your back over the left shoulder.

Step 3

Create 6 to 8 even pleats (approx. 5 inches wide) from the remaining fabric in the front and tuck them firmly into the waistband.

Step 4

Neatly arrange the shoulder pleats and secure with a cushioned safety pin behind your shoulder seam.

Bridal & Festive

2. Gujarati / Seedha Pallu Drape

Ideal for weddings and grand ceremonies as it brings the entire heavy zari pallu to the front chest, highlighting every woven motif.

Step 1

Complete the basic waist tuck from right to left as usual.

Step 2

Form pleats in the front and tuck them in, leaving the pallu end free.

Step 3

Take the pallu from the back over your right shoulder to the front, letting it fall vertically across your torso.

Step 4

Spread the intricate left corner of the pallu across your waist and secure it neatly on the left side of your hip.

Heritage Glamour

3. Bengali Regal Drape

A magnificent drape with wide box pleats and a sweeping double-pallu accent, perfect for Durga Puja, receptions, and family celebrations.

Step 1

Tuck the saree from right to left around the waist, then reverse and create 2 large broad box pleats in front.

Step 2

Take the pallu around your back and drape it generously over the left shoulder, letting it hang down.

Step 3

Pick the tip of the pallu, bring it under your right arm, and throw it back over the right shoulder with an ornate silver key-ring or brooch.

Modern Silhouette

4. Open Floating Pallu (Cocktail Style)

Let the sheer lightness of Organza or flow of Georgette Banarasi speak for itself with a fluid, unpleated open-arm pallu.

Step 1

Complete the waist tuck and tuck 5 to 6 neat pleats in front.

Step 2

Leave the pallu unpleated, pin only at the shoulder top, and let the sheer fabric drape fluidly over your forearm.

Step 3

Pair with a sleeveless or embroidered blouse for a contemporary royal look.
